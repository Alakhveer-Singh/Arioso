Desktop widgets

Lyrics and Recently Played live on your desktop. Drag them anywhere, resize them, and save the layout you like. And they're not just for looking.

Shown in Liquid Glass. Prefer solid cards filled with the song's moving album art? It's one switch in Settings.

Player

Always within reach.

A slim player sits at the bottom of your screen, on top of every app. Click it for shuffle, previous, play or pause, next and repeat, and drag the progress bar to skip around.

  • Waves that follow the musicOn macOS 14.2 and later, and with your permission, the bars move with the real loudness of Spotify or Apple Music. Arioso only measures it; nothing is recorded.
  • Never in your wayClicking it doesn't take focus from what you're typing in, and one switch puts it back on the desktop.
  • Made to fitMake it bigger or smaller with a slider, and choose frosted or clear glass, in Settings › Player.

Where the words come from

Quietly on your Mac.

Follows your player

By default Arioso only follows Spotify, so browser videos and other apps are ignored. Switch it off to follow whatever is playing: Music, a browser, anything.

FAQ

Before you press play.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac on macOS 13 Ventura or later. It's one universal app that runs natively on both Apple silicon and Intel.

Does it change how my Mac locks?

No. Arioso is a screen saver, so your Mac locks exactly as it always has and your password and Touch ID work the same. It just gives the lock screen something to sing.

What if a song has no lyrics?

If lrclib.net has timed lyrics, they play word by word. If it only has plain lyrics, those are shown instead. If there's nothing, you still get the album art, the song and the time.

Why does macOS say the app can't be opened?

Arioso isn't notarized by Apple yet. Run xattr -cr "/Applications/Arioso.app" once in Terminal, and from then on it opens like any other app.

Will Arioso update itself?

Yes. Once a day it checks this website for a newer version. When there is one, a window offers to install it and restart Arioso. You can turn the check off, or choose Check for Updates whenever you like, in Settings › About.

Does Arioso control my music?

Only when you ask it to: pressing a button on its player, clicking a lyric to jump, or clicking a song to play it again. Then it sends that one command to Spotify or Apple Music. It never changes playback on its own, and it never opens a music app itself.

Why does macOS ask to record audio?

So the player's waves can follow the real loudness of your music (macOS 14.2 and later). Arioso listens only to Spotify or Apple Music, only while a song plays, and turns each moment into a few numbers to move the bars. It keeps nothing and sends nothing. Say no, or turn off “Follow the music” in Settings › Player, and the waves simply animate instead.

Can I turn it all off?

Yes. One switch in Settings turns Arioso off: no widgets, no shortcut, and your Mac's normal lock screen. Turn it back on any time.

Will the widgets get in the way?

Drag and resize them anywhere, then save your layout. If things move around, "Back to my layout" snaps every widget back, and ⌘⇧L fits your windows into the space beside them.
